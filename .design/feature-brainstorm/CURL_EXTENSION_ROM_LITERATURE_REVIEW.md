# Curl / Extension "Depth/ROM" — Literature Review

Commissioned to fill the exact gap `EXERCISE_PARAMETERIZATION.md` §17 left unjustified: Depth/ROM was marked `N` for Curl and Extension with no reasoning given, unlike every other `N` in that table (which all got an explicit note — e.g. Fly's "N" on Sagittal, Leg Extension's blanket "N" row). This document is that missing reasoning, built from an actual search pass rather than assumed. `.design/feature-brainstorm/ROW_EMG_LITERATURE_REVIEW.md` does not exist yet in this repo, so there's no prior-art format to match — this follows the citation density and honesty conventions of the sourcing notes already in `EXERCISE_PARAMETERIZATION.md` §10 and §17-21 instead.

**Scope discipline, per the brief**: this is about **full ROM vs. partial ROM performed at the LENGTHENED/stretched end** of elbow flexion/extension — a completely different comparison from full-vs-shortened (top-half) partials, and a completely different question from "21s" as a specific 7+7+7 rep-scheme gimmick. All three get kept separate below; conflating them is exactly the mistake this review is trying to avoid.

---

## 1. Elbow flexion (biceps curls) — what the literature actually says

This is a real, active research area, not a gap. In rough chronological/rigor order:

- **Pinto et al. (2012), *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*.** Compared full-ROM biceps curls against partial-ROM curls performed in the **shortened** (top-half) position. Both groups grew elbow-flexor thickness with no significant between-group difference. Listed here for contrast only — this is the "less interesting" shortened-partial comparison the brief explicitly asked to exclude from the main analysis, not evidence about lengthened partials.

- **Kassiano et al. (2023), *Journal of Strength and Conditioning Research*ish (gastrocnemius calf-raise study, PubMed 37015016).** Not a curl study — the calf muscle — but this is the paper that put "lengthened partial ROM training" on the map as its own research question, and it's the direct methodological ancestor of every curl/triceps study below. 42 women, 8 weeks, 3x/week, full ROM vs. initial-portion (lengthened) partial ROM vs. final-portion (shortened) partial ROM calf raises. Medial gastrocnemius grew 15.2% in the lengthened-partial group vs. 6.7% full-ROM — a large, since-partially-walked-back effect (see Wolf 2025 below) that triggered the wider research push.

- **Kassiano et al. / related team, systematic review and meta-analysis, *Sport Sciences for Health* (2025).** Pooled 8 studies across vastus lateralis, rectus femoris, vastus medialis, vastus intermedius, gastrocnemius medialis/lateralis, **and biceps brachii/brachialis**. Finding: resistance training performed at longer muscle length produced significantly greater hypertrophy than shorter length overall, **ES = 0.283 (95% CI 0.04–0.52, p = 0.036)** — small but real, and directly inclusive of elbow flexors as one of the pooled muscle groups, not just legs/calves.

- **Zabaleta-Korta et al. (2023).** Compared incline curl (shoulder-extended, long-length-biased) vs. preacher curl (shoulder-flexed) — regional elbow-flexor hypertrophy differed by region, with preacher curl producing more distal-region growth. Important caveat: **this is a shoulder-position/length manipulation, not a same-exercise full-vs-partial-ROM manipulation** — it's evidence for the app's *existing* Sagittal Angle axis (§20 of `EXERCISE_PARAMETERIZATION.md`), not new evidence for a Depth/ROM axis. Cited for completeness, not counted toward the Depth/ROM case.

- **Havers et al. (2025), *European Journal of Sport Science*, "Partial Range, Full Gains? The Effect of 8 Weeks of Partial Range of Motion Training at Long Muscle Lengths on Elbow Flexor Hypertrophy and Strength in Trained Individuals."** 13 resistance-trained participants (11 men, 2 women), within-subject unilateral preacher curl design, one arm trained **initial/lengthened partial ROM (0°–70°)**, the other **full ROM (0°–140°)**, 8 weeks. Result: similar muscle-thickness gains at the mid-arm (50%-humeral-length) site; the lengthened-partial arm showed **trivial-to-small superiority** at the more distal 70% site. Directionally consistent with "lengthened partials are at least as good, possibly slightly better at some sites" — the effect is real but small, in an already-trained population where big between-condition swings are harder to produce anyway.

- **Wolf, Androulakis Korakakis, Piñero, Mohan, Hermann, Augustin, Sapuppo, Lin, Coleman, Burke, Nippard, Swinton & Schoenfeld (2025), *PeerJ* 13:e18904, "Lengthened partial repetitions elicit similar muscular adaptations as full range of motion repetitions during resistance training in trained individuals."** The single most relevant study for this app specifically: 30 healthy, resistance-trained participants, within-participant design (each person's own left/right arms randomized to condition), run as a **realistic multi-exercise training block** rather than one isolated preacher-curl-only protocol — closer to how Press's actual users train than a single-joint lab protocol. Covers **both elbow flexors and elbow extensors** (see §2 below). Bayesian analysis found **moderate evidence for the null (equivalence)** between lengthened-partial and full-ROM conditions across most measured sites for the elbow flexors. Billed by its own authors as "the most ecologically valid comparison of lengthened partial repetitions and full ROM resistance training to date."

**Net read for elbow flexion**: real, multi-study, meta-analyzed evidence exists. The honest synthesis (echoed by the "Stronger By Science" and "The Strength Equation" commentary threads that track this literature) is a **trend over time toward smaller effects as study rigor and training status increase** — early/less-trained-population studies (Kassiano's calf work, some of the earlier preprints) showed larger lengthened-partial advantages; the most recent, best-controlled study in trained lifters (Wolf 2025) finds **equivalence, not superiority**, and Havers 2025 finds only a trivial-to-small site-specific edge. **No study found lengthened partials producing worse outcomes than full ROM for elbow flexors.** That "never worse, sometimes trivially-to-modestly better, especially in less-trained populations" shape is a real, defensible, citable position — not nothing, but also not the dramatic "half reps are secretly better" claim gym folklore sometimes makes.

## 2. Elbow extension (triceps) — what the literature actually says

Thinner than the curl literature, and what exists splits into two genuinely different questions that are easy to conflate:

- **Maeo et al. (2023), *European Journal of Sport Science*, "Triceps brachii hypertrophy is substantially greater after elbow extension training performed in the overhead versus neutral arm position."** 21 adults, 12 weeks, 2x/week, cable extensions in **overhead vs. neutral-arm** position. Overhead (long head under stretch, since it's biarticular and crosses the shoulder) produced **~40% greater triceps growth** than the neutral/pushdown-style position, including greater medial/lateral head growth too, not just long head. **This is the study already cited in this codebase** for Carter Extension and Overhead Tricep Extension's `curveNote`s. It is genuinely a stretch-mediated-hypertrophy finding — but it's a **shoulder-position (length-via-biarticular-stretch) manipulation**, exactly the same axis type as Curl's Sagittal Angle (§21 of `EXERCISE_PARAMETERIZATION.md` already models this via `ELBOW_SHOULDER_MODIFIER`), **not** a same-exercise full-vs-partial-rep-depth manipulation. It answers "does shoulder position change triceps-long-head length/growth" (yes, already modeled), not "does doing only the bottom half of a pushdown rep beat doing the whole rep" (a different question, thinner evidence — see below).
- **Wolf et al. (2025) PeerJ 13:e18904 (same study as §1)** is the one source found that actually asks the on-topic question for triceps: it explicitly includes **elbow extensors** in its multi-exercise lengthened-partial-vs-full-ROM comparison. Result: "moderate evidence in support of the null hypothesis across sites, with the exception of the elbow extensor triceps brachii 55% site, which showed **anecdotal evidence in support of the null**" — i.e., equivalence at every measured triceps site, with the weakest (still null-favoring) evidence at one specific site. No superiority signal for lengthened partials on triceps specifically was found here.
- **No dedicated single-exercise triceps trial** (e.g., a pushdown-only or skullcrusher-only full-vs-lengthened-partial RCT, independent of shoulder position) turned up in this search. The triceps-specific ROM literature is overwhelmingly about the overhead-vs-neutral shoulder-position question (Maeo, and this codebase's own prior citations), which the app has already correctly modeled as Sagittal Angle. The actual same-exercise-depth question rides almost entirely on Wolf 2025's inclusion of extensor exercises inside its broader multi-joint protocol, not a triceps-dedicated study.

**Net read for elbow extension**: weaker and more indirect than the curl case. The strong, well-replicated triceps finding (Maeo, 40% effect) is about an axis this app already has (shoulder position/Sagittal Angle), not about Depth/ROM. The actual Depth/ROM-relevant finding (Wolf 2025) shows equivalence, same as elbow flexion, but is one study rather than a meta-analyzed body of evidence the way flexion has.

## 3. EMG-activation findings specifically at the stretched position

The brief asked for this separately from hypertrophy outcomes, and it's worth being precise about what does and doesn't exist:

- Multiple studies confirm **within-rep, angle-dependent EMG** for both muscles — e.g. biceps activation in preacher curls peaking near full elbow extension (the stretched end), and the medial triceps head showing significantly greater EMG in "the terminal 30° arc" of elbow extension (the locked-out end, which is the *shortened* position for triceps, opposite of biceps' stretched end — worth not confusing the two, since "stretched" is exercise-direction-dependent: it's the bottom of a curl but the top-ish/mid-range of an extension movement, not the lockout).
- What was **not found**: a study directly comparing **mean/total EMG amplitude of a lengthened-partial-ROM training protocol vs. a full-ROM protocol** (i.e., "does training only the stretched portion produce higher cumulative muscle activation across a whole set than training the full range") for either curls or extensions. The existing EMG literature answers "which joint angle has the highest instantaneous activation within one rep" (a within-rep question, already the kind of thing `movementEmg.js`'s `ELBOW_EMG`/`ELBOW_ANGLES` per-rep table tracks), not "which multi-week training protocol produces more stimulus" (the between-protocol question this whole review is about). These are related but distinct, and conflating them would overstate the evidence. Flagging this gap explicitly rather than papering over it.

## 4. Does this clear the bar to add a real Depth/ROM parameter?

**Short answer: the literature itself clears the evidence bar — arguably more cleanly than several axes already built (grip-width's row data is a single two-point study; the resistance-curve ordinal scale in §2/§7 is explicitly "best-effort, no-citation-required"). But it doesn't cleanly fit the *mechanism* the existing curl/extension axes use, and that mismatch — not thin evidence — is the real reason to hold off on a straightforward port of the Sagittal Angle pattern.**

Here's the mismatch, spelled out:

- **Sagittal Angle and Rotation both work by redistributing EMG credit across competing muscles at the same total effort** — `ELBOW_SHOULDER_MODIFIER` shifts credit between biceps long/short heads (and by extension biceps vs. brachialis/brachioradialis) as shoulder position changes; that's exactly the kind of table `matchExerciseName`/the isolation-ratio recommendation formula (§5) is built to consume.
- **The Depth/ROM literature does not produce a redistribution table.** Its headline finding, across both flexion and extension, is **equivalence in total hypertrophy** between full and lengthened-partial ROM, with at most a small, population-dependent (bigger in less-trained lifters, shrinking toward zero in trained ones) overall advantage — not "biceps gets more credit and brachialis gets less" the way angle/rotation do. The one place there's a *regional* effect (Havers 2025's distal-site edge) is about **which part of the same muscle grows more** (proximal vs. distal biceps), which this app's muscle-crediting model has no concept of at all — `muscleCapacity.js` and `fatigue.js` credit whole named muscles (biceps, brachialis, brachioradialis), not sub-regions of one muscle. Building a real regional-hypertrophy model would be a materially bigger, separate feature, not a slot-in to the existing EMG-percentage mechanism.
- So even with citable, meta-analyzed evidence in hand, there's no honest way to fill in "X% credit to biceps, Y% to brachialis, Z% to brachioradialis" for full vs. lengthened-partial the way §20's angle table does — because the actual finding is "roughly the same total credit, redistributed within one muscle in a way this app doesn't track," not "more credit to a different named muscle."

**Recommendation: don't build it as a stored, EMG-crediting parameter the way Sagittal Angle/Rotation/Width work.** That would either (a) fabricate a redistribution the literature doesn't support, or (b) require a genuinely new regional-hypertrophy modeling capability this app doesn't have anywhere else, which is a much bigger and separately-scoped piece of work than "add a picker field." Recommend instead:

- **A coaching-note-level acknowledgment, not a stored/credited parameter** — same tier as the existing advisory-only `gripCueForProfile` before §11 promoted rotation to a real parameter, except this one should probably **stay** advisory rather than following rotation's path, because (per above) there's no honest percentage table to promote it *to*. Something like: an athlete who's logged the same curl/extension variant for a long stretch could get an Atlas-voice note mentioning that deliberately emphasizing the bottom/stretched portion of the rep is a real, evidence-backed technique variation (cite Wolf 2025 / the meta-analysis) worth rotating in — informational, matching `PRODUCT.md`'s "editorial, not a black box" stance, not a claim that it changes muscle credit.
- **If a stored field is wanted anyway** (e.g. for the athlete's own training log fidelity, independent of whether it feeds the fatigue/capacity model): a simple **Full / Lengthened-Partial** binary toggle, per-exercise-instance, same storage convention as every other field in §6 — deliberately not more granular than that, since the literature doesn't support finer distinctions (there's no evidence base for, say, "bottom-third" vs. "bottom-half" as separately meaningful categories). This would be closer to a logging annotation than a muscle-crediting input, and should be built (if at all) with that framing made explicit, not silently treated as EMG-bearing data the way angle/rotation/width are.

This is the same kind of "real evidence, wrong shape for this app's mechanism" call the doc already made elsewhere (§12's Stance/Support fatigue-crediting mechanism was proposed but explicitly deferred as its own harder problem, not bundled into the Phase 2 ship). Depth/ROM should get the same treatment: **flagged as evidenced, not silently built into the EMG table.**

### If it does get built (scope, since the brief asked)

No new named exercises needed. The toggle is orthogonal to shoulder position, equipment, and rotation — a barbell curl, a preacher curl, and a cable pushdown can each be done full-ROM or lengthened-partial regardless of their existing angle/rotation values — so it would apply as an optional field across essentially the whole existing curl/extension roster in `functions/exerciseDb.js` (`barbell-curl`, `ez-bar-curl`, `dumbbell-curl-standing`, `incline-dumbbell-curl`, `hammer-curl`, `preacher-curl-barbell`, `spider-curl`, `decline-curl`, `low-cable-curl`, `drag-curl`, `concentration-curl`, `cross-body-hammer-curl`, `zottman-curl`, `reverse-curl` for Curl; `cable-pushdown-rope`, `cable-pushdown-bar`, `skullcrusher-barbell`, `skullcrusher-ez`, `overhead-tricep-extension-cable`, `overhead-tricep-extension-dumbbell`, `carter-extension` for Extension), not a subset. `jm-press` and `tricep-dips` are the closest things to exceptions worth a second look before including — JM Press is a hybrid compound-ish movement and Tricep Dips is already carved out of the isolation-extension list per §21, so both would need the same "is this genuinely isolation enough for a rep-depth toggle to mean anything" gut-check the rest of the list doesn't need.

## 5. "21s" specifically — separate question, owner's rejection confirmed

No peer-reviewed or otherwise rigorous study of the **specific 7-bottom/7-top/7-full rep-scheme** was found anywhere in this search. Every source that discusses "21s" by name is gym-culture material — technique-guide blogs (fitnessprogramer.com, setforset.com, steelsupplements.com, sweat.com), a bodybuilding-history piece tracing it to Arnold Schwarzenegger's *Encyclopedia of Modern Bodybuilding* (1985), and marketing copy that gestures at "mechanical stress and metabolic fatigue" and "time under tension" without citing anything. None of it references an actual comparative trial of the 21s protocol against straight sets, against lengthened-partials-only training, or against anything else.

This **confirms the app owner's rejection is correct, and for the right reason**: 21s isn't "debunked" by any study either (nothing rigorous enough exists to debunk it) — it's simply **never been studied as its own protocol**, full stop. It's folklore in the specific sense of "an untested, name-brand technique," not in the sense of "a tested-and-failed technique." That's a meaningfully different and more defensible position than either "science says 21s works" or "science says 21s is a myth" — the honest statement is "no science exists on 21s specifically."

Critically, this is fully separable from §1-4 above: the broader lengthened-partials literature (Kassiano, Havers, Wolf, the 2025 meta-analysis) is real, growing, and reasonably rigorous — it just has nothing to do with 21s's particular fixed 7+7+7 structure. A lifter doing pure lengthened-partial reps for an entire working set (what the literature actually studies) is doing something structurally different from 21s's three-segment single-set scheme (partial-bottom, then partial-top, then full, all in one set) — the literature doesn't validate 21s by association, and shouldn't be cited as if it does. If this app ever surfaces anything about lengthened partials (per §4's coaching-note recommendation), it should be careful not to let that read as retroactive vindication of 21s specifically.

## 6. Summary table

| Question | Finding |
|---|---|
| Does real literature compare lengthened-partial vs. full ROM for curls? | Yes — Pinto 2012 (contrast case only), Kassiano 2023 (calf, methodological ancestor), 2025 meta-analysis (ES=0.283, small, sig., includes biceps/brachialis), Havers 2025 (trivial-small edge to partial at one site), Wolf 2025 (equivalence in trained lifters). |
| Does real literature compare lengthened-partial vs. full ROM for triceps extension? | Thinner — Maeo 2023's 40% effect is a shoulder-position (already-modeled Sagittal Angle) finding, not a same-exercise depth finding; Wolf 2025 is the one on-topic source, showing equivalence. |
| Any case where lengthened partials were WORSE than full ROM? | None found, for either muscle group. |
| Direct EMG comparison of partial-ROM vs. full-ROM protocols (not just within-rep angle curves)? | Not found — this specific comparison doesn't appear to exist yet in the literature. |
| Does the evidence support a new stored, EMG-crediting Depth/ROM parameter (matching Sagittal Angle's rigor and mechanism)? | No — evidence is real but shows equivalence/regional effects the existing whole-muscle EMG-redistribution mechanism can't honestly represent. Recommend coaching-note-tier acknowledgment, or at most a non-EMG-bearing logging toggle, not a promoted parameter. |
| Is "21s" (the specific 7+7+7 scheme) studied anywhere? | No — zero rigorous sources found. Owner's rejection is correct: not debunked, simply never studied as its own protocol, and not the same thing as the (real) general lengthened-partials literature. |

**Bottom line for §17's table**: Depth/ROM's `N` for Curl/Extension should change from "unexplained" to **explicitly justified as `N` for the EMG-crediting mechanism specifically** — the literature exists and is reasonably good, but it doesn't produce the kind of muscle-to-muscle redistribution table that column's `Y`/`N` has meant everywhere else in that schema. Worth a follow-up product decision on the coaching-note-only version, but that's a `§3`-style feature (informational layer), not a `§17`-style stored parameter.
